MOTIVATION
Population is one of the simplest headline numbers about a country, yet the world is far from evenly split. A handful of giants hold most of humanity. This ps takes a quick look at how lopsided the distribution is, checks whether two independent sources agree, and pairs it with a small US health-survey sample to see how skew shows up inside one country too.

RESEARCH QUESTIONS
1. How is population distributed across countries: dominated by a few giants, or spread out fairly evenly?
2. Do Wikipedia (UN table) and the World Bank API roughly agree on the ranking of the largest countries?
3. Inside the US, do age and household-income (poverty ratio) differ between men and women in a small NHANES slice?

HYPOTHESES
H1: Population is heavily right-skewed. Mean is much larger than median because China, India, and the US dominate.
H2: Wikipedia and the World Bank agree closely on the top ranks. Small gaps come from year and methodology.
H3: In a 50-person random NHANES draw, age and poverty ratio differ only modestly across genders.

UNIT OF ANALYSIS
Datasets 1 and 2 (HTML, JSON): one row = one country.
Dataset 3 (SAS): one row = one NHANES survey respondent.
Primary unit: country.

DATASETS
1. HTML: Wikipedia, "List of countries by population (UN)"
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population_(United_Nations)
   Top 50 kept. Cols: country, population, change_pct, region.

2. JSON: World Bank population indicator (SP.POP.TOTL), 2022
   https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/all/indicator/SP.POP.TOTL?format=json&date=2022&per_page=400
   ~200 sovereign countries. Cols: country, iso3, population.

3. SAS xpt: CDC NHANES 2021-2023 Demographics (DEMO_L)
   https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2021/DataFiles/DEMO_L.xpt
   Random sample of 50 respondents. Cols: id, gender, age, race, poverty_ratio.

WHY THESE DATASETS
Small, public, and free to redistribute. Three different formats (HTML, JSON, SAS xpt) as the assignment requires. Population is intuitive so the stats are easy to interpret; NHANES adds a person-level view without extra complexity.

LIMITATIONS
- Top-50 slice on the Wikipedia table hides the long tail of small countries.
- 50-person NHANES sample is too small for real inference; proper analysis would use survey weights (WTINT2YR).
- No fuzzy country-name matching between Wikipedia and World Bank. Kept simple on purpose.
